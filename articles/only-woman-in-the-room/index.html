On being the only woman in the room

18 JUNE 2026 · BY AMARA HOLT · 6 MIN

And how I stopped letting that define the work.

The first time I counted, there were nineteen of us in the room and I was the only one. I know because I counted twice, which tells you roughly where my attention was for the first forty minutes of that meeting.

What I thought it meant

For a long time I read the count as a verdict on whether I belonged. It took an embarrassingly long time to notice that the count is a fact about the room, not about me.

  • It says something about who got hired
  • It says something about who stayed
  • It says nothing at all about who is right in a design review

What I do now

I still notice. I just don’t spend the meeting on it. The work is the same work it would be in any other room, and it turns out to be the only part of this I have much control over.

That’s the whole trick, as far as I have found one. Not to stop seeing it, but to stop letting the seeing take up the space the work needs.
